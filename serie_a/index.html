Analysis

So — who performed well,
and who didn't?

Serie A 2023–24 was defined by Inter Milan's dominant Scudetto campaign and Napoli's stunning collapse from champions to mid-table. The physical data maps neither story cleanly. Distance tells you how much. It cannot tell you why.

Case 01 · Champions Through Control

Inter Milan won the Scudetto by 19 points averaging 10,921 m per player per match — 7th lowest in the league. Their HSR distance of 495 m ranked them 14th.

Structural dominance, not physical dominance.

Case 02 · Atalanta's Intensity

Atalanta led Serie A in total distance (11,342 m), HSR distance (558 m), and sprint distance (289 m). Under Gian Piero Gasperini, physical intensity is doctrine.

They finished 5th — and won the Europa League.

Case 03 · Napoli's Collapse

Napoli, champions the season before, ranked 18th in total distance (10,684 m) and last in sprint distance. Three managers, a fractured squad, and the lowest physical output in the top half of the table.

They finished 10th.

Case 04 · Relegated from the Bottom

Salernitana finished last — 55 points from safety — and also ranked last in total distance (10,474 m) and sprint distance (205 m). Here, the physical floor and the sporting floor aligned.

The exception that hints at a correlation that doesn't hold universally.

What the data cannot tell you

Physical metrics are necessary. But on their own, they answer the wrong question. A player running 12,000 m in a match where their team concedes four goals has produced very different value from a player running 10,500 m in a 1–0 win.

Data must be accurate first. It must make sense second — situated within tactical context. And it must be contextualised with the people involved: the head of performance who knows why a player's sprint numbers dropped, the analyst who spotted that the HSR spike came from chasing lost games.

Analysis

So — who performed well,
and who didn't?

Serie A 2023–24 was defined by Inter's dominant Scudetto and Napoli's stunning collapse. The physical data maps neither story cleanly. Distance tells you how much. It cannot tell you why.

Case 01 · Champions Through Control

Inter Milan won the Scudetto by 19 points averaging 10,921 m per player per match — 7th lowest in the league. Their HSR distance of 495 m ranked them 14th.

Structural dominance. Not physical dominance.

Case 02 · Atalanta's Intensity

Atalanta led Serie A in total distance (11,342 m), HSR (558 m), and sprint distance (289 m). Under Gasperini, physical intensity is doctrine.

They finished 5th — and won the Europa League.

Case 03 · Napoli's Collapse

Napoli, defending champions, ranked 18th in total distance (10,684 m) and last in sprint distance. Three managers, a fractured squad, and the lowest physical output among contenders.

They finished 10th.

Case 04 · When the Floors Align

Salernitana finished last — 55 points from safety — and also ranked last in total distance (10,474 m) and sprint distance (205 m).

The exception that hints at a correlation that doesn't hold universally.

What the data cannot tell you

Physical metrics are necessary. But on their own, they answer the wrong question. A player running 12,000 m in a match where their team concedes four goals has produced very different value from one running 10,500 m in a 1–0 win. The number looks better. The outcome is worse.

Data must be accurate first — collected cleanly, standardised across systems. It must make sense second — placed within the tactical and seasonal context of each match. And it must be contextualised with the people involved: the performance coach who knows why a player's numbers dropped in March, the analyst who flagged that the HSR spike came from chasing games in the final minutes.

Analysis

So — who performed well,
and who didn't?

It seems like a simple question. But if you look at the numbers alone, the honest answer is: it's impossible to say.

Case 01 · Effort ≠ Result

Burnley averaged 11,608 m per player per match — the highest in the league. They also recorded the highest sprint distance: 284 m per player per match above 25.2 km/h. By every physical metric, they were the hardest-working side in the Premier League.

They were relegated in 19th place.

Case 02 · Economy of Effort

Man City averaged 11,342 m per player per match — well below the league average of ~11,450 m. Their HSR distance of 511 m per player per match ranked them mid-table for high-intensity output.

They won the title.

Case 03 · The Explosive Mid-Table

Tottenham led the league in sprint distance with 305 m per player per match — over 20 m more per player per game than anyone else. Their HSR distance of 584 m was also the highest in the division.

They finished 5th — strong, but not exceptional given that output.

Case 04 · Low Intensity, High Return

Aston Villa recorded the lowest HSR distance in the top eight: 453 m per player per match. Their total distance of 10,868 m ranked them among the least physically active sides in the league.

They finished 4th and qualified for the Champions League.

What the data cannot tell you

Physical metrics are necessary. But on their own, they answer the wrong question. A player running 12,000 m in a match where their team concedes four goals has produced very different value from a player running 10,500 m in a 1–0 win. The number looks better. The outcome is worse.

Data must be accurate first — collected cleanly, standardised across systems, free of noise. It must make sense second — situated within the tactical context of each match, each opponent, each phase of the season. And it must be contextualised with the people involved: the head of performance who knows why a player's sprint numbers dropped in March, the analyst who spotted that the HSR spike in December came from chasing games in the final ten minutes.

This is where syndeoo operates. We bridge the gap between raw physical output and meaningful decision-making — integrating data from tracking systems, from staff, and from the field, and presenting it in a form that makes the next call faster and more certain.

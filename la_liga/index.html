Analysis

So — who performed well,
and who didn't?

La Liga 2023–24 delivered a three-way title race with Girona emerging as the most surprising story in European football. The physical data complicates any simple narrative about who worked hardest — and whether it mattered. The numbers alone cannot give you the answer.

Case 01 · Champions with Economy

Real Madrid averaged 11,000 m per player per match — the fourth-lowest total in the league. Their sprint distance of 258 m ranked them 16th of 20 clubs.

They won La Liga by 10 points.

Case 02 · Girona's Remarkable Season

Girona led the league in total distance at 11,447 m per player per match, and ranked 3rd in both HSR and sprint distance. Their physical output was extraordinary for a promoted side.

They finished 3rd — Champions League qualification in their first-ever top-flight European campaign.

Case 03 · Effort at the Bottom

Rayo Vallecano ranked 2nd in total distance (11,289 m) and led the league mid-season in sprint count. Their willingness to press and recover was evident across all metrics.

They finished 15th — just four points above the relegation zone.

Case 04 · Relegated Despite Running

Granada and Almeria, both relegated, finished bottom two in every physical category — with Granada at 10,579 m per player and Almeria at 10,500 m.

In La Liga, the physical floor matters — but it doesn't guarantee survival.

What the data cannot tell you

Physical metrics are necessary. But on their own, they answer the wrong question. A player running 12,000 m in a match where their team concedes four goals has produced very different value from a player running 10,500 m in a 1–0 win.

Data must be accurate first. It must make sense second — situated within tactical context. And it must be contextualised with the people involved: the head of performance who knows why a player's sprint numbers dropped, the analyst who spotted that the HSR spike came from chasing lost games.

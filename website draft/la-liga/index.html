Analysis

So — who performed well,
and who didn't?

La Liga 2023–24 produced a three-way title race and Girona's most extraordinary season. The physical data complicates any simple narrative. The numbers alone cannot give you the answer.

Case 01 · Champions with Economy

Real Madrid averaged 11,000 m per player per match — the fourth-lowest in the league. Sprint distance of 258 m ranked them 16th of 20.

They won La Liga by 10 points.

Case 02 · Girona's Breakthrough Season

Girona led the league in total distance at 11,447 m per player and ranked 3rd in both HSR and sprint distance. Extraordinary output for a promoted side.

They finished 3rd — Champions League qualification in their first-ever European campaign.

Case 03 · Effort Without Reward

Rayo Vallecano ranked 2nd in total distance (11,289 m) and led mid-season in sprint count. Their pressing effort was clear across every metric.

They finished 15th — four points above the relegation zone.

Case 04 · The Physical Floor

Granada and Almeria, both relegated, ranked last in every physical category — Granada at 10,579 m per player, Almeria at 10,500 m.

In La Liga, the physical floor matters — but it doesn't guarantee survival on its own.

What the data cannot tell you

Physical metrics are necessary. But on their own, they answer the wrong question. A player running 12,000 m in a match where their team concedes four goals has produced very different value from one running 10,500 m in a 1–0 win. The number looks better. The outcome is worse.

Data must be accurate first — collected cleanly, standardised across systems. It must make sense second — placed within the tactical and seasonal context of each match. And it must be contextualised with the people involved: the performance coach who knows why a player's numbers dropped in March, the analyst who flagged that the HSR spike came from chasing games in the final minutes.

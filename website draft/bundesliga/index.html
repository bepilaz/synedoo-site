Analysis

So — who performed well,
and who didn't?

The 2023–24 Bundesliga produced one of the most extraordinary seasons in European football history. The physical data raises the same question: the numbers alone cannot tell you who performed well and who didn't.

Case 01 · The Unbeaten Champions

Bayer Leverkusen went the entire season undefeated — 28 wins, 6 draws. Their total distance of ~11,212 m per player per match sat below the league average.

The most dominant season in Bundesliga history — achieved with below-average physical volume.

Case 02 · High Output, Modest Return

Hoffenheim ranked highest in total distance at 11,824 m per player per match, and led the league in HSR distance. Their effort was unmatched.

They finished 7th.

Case 03 · Leipzig's Pressing Machine

RB Leipzig led in sprint distance at 288 m per player per match and ranked highest in HSR. The most physically demanding profile in the division.

They finished 4th — 25 points behind Leverkusen.

Case 04 · Speed Without Structure

Darmstadt, relegated with 17 points, produced sprint distances competitive with mid-table sides — above both Leverkusen and Bayern in this metric.

They conceded 90 goals and finished last.

What the data cannot tell you

Physical metrics are necessary. But on their own, they answer the wrong question. A player running 12,000 m in a match where their team concedes four goals has produced very different value from one running 10,500 m in a 1–0 win. The number looks better. The outcome is worse.

Data must be accurate first — collected cleanly, standardised across systems. It must make sense second — placed within the tactical and seasonal context of each match. And it must be contextualised with the people involved: the performance coach who knows why a player's numbers dropped in March, the analyst who flagged that the HSR spike came from chasing games in the final minutes.

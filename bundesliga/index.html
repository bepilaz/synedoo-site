Analysis

So — who performed well,
and who didn't?

The 2023–24 Bundesliga produced one of the most extraordinary seasons in European football history. Yet the data raises the same uncomfortable question it always does: the numbers alone cannot tell you who performed well and who didn't.

Case 01 · The Unbeaten Champions

Bayer Leverkusen went the entire season undefeated — 28 wins, 6 draws. Their total distance of ~11,212 m per player per match sat below the league average.

They won the title by 6 points. The most dominant season in Bundesliga history — with below-average physical volume.

Case 02 · High Output, Modest Return

Hoffenheim ranked highest in total distance at 11,824 m per player per match, and led the league in HSR distance. Their effort levels were unmatched.

They finished 7th.

Case 03 · Leipzig's Pressing Machine

RB Leipzig ranked highest in sprint distance at 288 m per player per match, and led in HSR. Their high-intensity profile was the most demanding in the division.

They finished 4th — 25 points behind Leverkusen.

Case 04 · Explosive but Relegated

Darmstadt, relegated with just 17 points, produced sprint distances competitive with established mid-table sides — above Leverkusen and Bayern in this metric.

Speed without structure. They conceded 90 goals and finished last.

What the data cannot tell you

Physical metrics are necessary. But on their own, they answer the wrong question. A player running 12,000 m in a match where their team concedes four goals has produced very different value from a player running 10,500 m in a 1–0 win.

Data must be accurate first. It must make sense second — situated within tactical context. And it must be contextualised with the people involved: the head of performance who knows why a player's sprint numbers dropped, the analyst who spotted that the HSR spike came from chasing lost games.
